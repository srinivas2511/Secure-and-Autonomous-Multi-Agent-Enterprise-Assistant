# Disciplinary Procedure

**Purpose**: This procedure ensures that employee conduct and performance issues are addressed fairly, consistently, and in compliance with applicable employment law.

**Scope**: Applies to all full-time, part-time, and fixed-term employees. Contractors are governed by their individual agreements.

**Progressive discipline steps**:
1. **Verbal warning** — documented by the manager in the HR system within 24 hours. Employee acknowledges receipt. Remains active for 6 months.
2. **Written warning** — formal written notice detailing the issue, required improvement, timeline, and consequences. HR must review before issuance. Employee signs to acknowledge; refusal to sign is itself documented.
3. **Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)** — a structured 30–90 day plan with specific, measurable milestones. HR Business Partner co-owns the PIP alongside the manager. Weekly check-ins are mandatory.
4. **Final written warning** — issued when a PIP is failed or a serious new incident occurs during an active warning. Specifies that further violations may result in termination.
5. **Termination** — requires written approval from the department head and HR Director before any communication to the employee.

**Summary dismissal**: Certain offences may bypass progressive steps and result in immediate termination. These include: fraud, theft, workplace violence, harassment after a formal finding, disclosure of confidential information to competitors, and serious safety violations.

**Investigation process**: Allegations requiring investigation are assigned to an HR Business Partner within one business day. The subject employee may be placed on paid administrative leave during the investigation. Investigations are concluded within 15 business days where possible.

**Records retention**: All disciplinary records are kept in the confidential HR file for seven years and are accessible only to HR and the employee's management chain.
